Strategy (MSTR) Q3 2026 — Fewer Coins a Share, Fewer Claims Ahead

Strategy's stock rose 76% in the third quarter against 43% for bitcoin, and the company sold $5.41 billion of common stock. It put 11% of that into bitcoin. The rest retired preferred stock and filled a dollar reserve. Each share now holds 11.8% less bitcoin than in June and has 34% fewer dollars of net senior claims ahead of it (debt and preferred, less its dollars and software). Rolled forward on July's method, fair value rises from $94 to $124.79, almost all of it from the bitcoin price. Four changes to the method, each shown with its own effect, net to −$0.18: the preferred and the convertibles at market value, a tax on the unrealized gain, and dilution from employee awards. Fair value is $124.61. The $141.62 close of 29 October is 13.6% above that, a premium of 1.14× to net asset value.

TON618 Capital Research · As of 29 October 2026 · Download PDF · Download model

In July we valued Strategy at its net asset value, about $94 a share: bitcoin at spot, plus cash and the software business, less the convertible notes and the preferred stock. We gave the preferred-stock franchise no value of its own, because the premium that would make new issuance pay had gone. The third quarter tested that call from both sides. The premium came back, from 1.04 times NAV at the end of June to 1.13 times at the end of September, and the company sold more common stock than in any quarter this year. But it bought little bitcoin with it. It used the proceeds to buy back its 12% preferred below par, build $5.07 billion of dollar assets by 25 October, and sell no new credit at all. This note asks what that trade did to each share, and what $141.62 pays for.

Terms used throughout, defined once. Net asset value (NAV): bitcoin at the market price, plus Strategy's dollar assets and the software business at a comparables value, less the convertible notes and the preferred stock at their market values, and a 21% federal tax on the unrealized gain on the bitcoin. Our fair value is NAV per share at a premium of 1.00×. Like-for-like basis: where an exhibit compares two dates (the quarter's bridge, coin per share, the trade, the funding paths, the strike and wipe-out levels), the preferred is at liquidation preference and the convertibles at principal on both dates, no tax is deducted, and the exhibit says so. mNAV: price divided by NAV per share. The company's own figure leaves out the software and uses the preferred's notional amount. Two bars for selling stock. Above NAV per share ($124.61), new common adds net asset value per share. Above coin value per share (bitcoin held times the bitcoin price, divided by basic shares, $157.10), it also adds bitcoin per share. The note names the bar each statement uses. Both are the company's own: its presentation says that at an mNAV above one, new stock that buys bitcoin "would be expected to increase Net Bitcoin Per Share", and its BTC Yield measures the gross bitcoin per share. Net coin per share: NAV divided by the bitcoin price and the share count, the bitcoin each share would own after every senior claim. USD assets: the company's USD Reserve plus USD Cash. BTC Yield: the company's percentage change in bitcoin per assumed-diluted share, a measure that, by the company's own description, does not deduct the senior claims. Shares: basic class A and B shares, including those sold after the balance-sheet date, plus employee options and stock units by the treasury-stock method (425.4 million in all). All six convertible notes are out of the money at this price, so none is counted as converted in the base case.

The case for the price

The case for the price, in the company's terms

Strategy's case starts from its balance sheet. The release says the company "returned STRC to health" by buying back STRC and growing its USD assets. Net leverage is 2.38%, from 8.4% in the second quarter, on its own measure: debt less USD assets, over bitcoin. USD assets of $5.07 billion cover 3.4 years of interest and dividends, a coverage period the company says rose nearly seven times from its May low. On the company's arithmetic bitcoin could fall to $17,662 before the coin alone stops covering the debt and preferred net of those dollars. STRC, the flagship preferred, closed at $99.99 on 29 October, a cent from its $100 stated amount, after 20.98 million shares were bought back at an average $97.39. From 1 November STRC pays a dividend for every calendar day. The release adds that distributions on its preferred equity instruments are expected to be return of capital for tax purposes "for the foreseeable future (i.e., ten years or more)", which raises their after-tax yield for a taxable holder.

The forward claim is that this foundation lets credit, not equity, do the buying. The company's stated objective is "Doubling BPS in 7 Years Through Digital Credit": bitcoin per share up about 10.4% a year. On the call Michael Saylor said the company could create $10 billion to $30 billion of digital credit now, with its existing capital structure, without raising more equity and without bitcoin rising. Its own hurdle is that bitcoin must rise 10.6% a year for that credit to add value to the common, and 2.2% a year to cover the dividends from the coin. The premium is the market's price for that option. The company's chart shows median daily mNAV of 2.28× in 2021, 2.57× in 2023 and 2.60× in 2024, against 1.15× now. The record behind the objective is long. On the company's own measure, bitcoin per share compounded about 23% a year from the end of 2020 to 25 October, from 56,598 to 187,753 sats. From bitcoin's all-time high in October 2025, the company's holdings rose 33% to 25 October while the price fell 35% to 23 October. This note tests the last four months of that record, not the six years. Michael Saylor wrote in the release: "We're at the beginning of the next bitcoin bull market." Read this way, $141.62 is cheap: a credit engine about to restart, on a balance sheet now built for a downturn.

This note tests three of these claims. First, whether the quarter's issuance added bitcoin per share (it did not; §1, §2). Second, what the repair cost the common and what it bought (§2, §3). Third, what the premium is paying for (§4). It cannot test the claim on which the case finally rests: whether bitcoin will beat the 10.6% hurdle over the years the credit is outstanding. That is a forecast of bitcoin, and the house does not make one. It is carried as scenarios and as the tripwires in §6, not as a verdict.

The print

What the quarter did: the price outran bitcoin, not net asset value

The release reports operating income of $20.91 billion for the quarter and diluted earnings of $44.75 a share. Almost all of it is the $20.91 billion fair-value gain on bitcoin. Software revenue was $125.4 million, down 3%, at a 71% gross margin. Bitcoin rose 43% between the quarter-end prices, and MSTR rose 76%. The gap between the two has two sources, and the filed balance sheets separate them.

The flywheel is a different question: did issuance at a premium turn into more bitcoin per share? It did not. The company sold $5.41 billion of class A stock and no preferred. Shares outstanding rose from 371.6 million to 422.0 million, or 13.6%, in one quarter. About 50.4 million shares were added in all, including a small number from equity awards, at an average $107.35. At the quarter's average bitcoin price, $71,171, NAV per share on June's balances was $112.70. The common was therefore sold at roughly 0.95× NAV, and at 0.66× its coin value per share. Below 1.0× coin value, a dollar of new stock buys less bitcoin than the share it creates already owns. Every coin bought with it lowers bitcoin per share. The proceeds went elsewhere:

The quarter's issuance was not bitcoin accumulation. It was a balance-sheet repair paid for in common stock.

The trade

The trade: bitcoin per share for claims per share

The company's own measure records the cost. BTC Yield was −7.8% from the second-quarter print to 25 October. Year to date it fell from +4.5% to −3.7%, at 187,753 sats a share on about 452 million assumed-diluted shares. On basic shares the fall is larger. Each share held 227,662 sats of bitcoin on 30 June and 200,910 on 25 October, −11.8%. Holdings grew 0.3% over those four months and basic shares 13.7%.

That is half of the trade. The other half is what sits ahead of each share. Convertible principal and preferred liquidation preference, less dollars and the software value, came to $49.33 a share on 30 June and $32.31 on 25 October, −34%. The preferred buybacks and the reserve did that. The common now owns fewer coins, with fewer dollars owed ahead of it. Whether the share came out ahead depends on the bitcoin price at which the claims are counted. A fixed-dollar claim is a smaller bite of the stack when bitcoin is high and a larger one when it is low.

The path from here, with no bitcoin return assumed. Hold the bitcoin price, the share price and the premium where they closed, and fund the $1.51 billion annual fixed charge four ways. From the reserve, as the company now does, bitcoin per share holds flat and NAV per share falls 2.9% a year, the charge leaving as dollars. With new common at $141.62, bitcoin per share falls 2.5% a year. With bitcoin sales it falls 2.3%. Repeat the third quarter's pattern for a year ($5.41 billion of common a quarter, 11% of it to coin) and NAV per share edges up +1.5%, because stock sold above NAV adds to it. Bitcoin per share falls 24%. No funding path raises gross bitcoin per share at this price; issuing above NAV does raise NAV per share, which is the other bar. Doing so takes common sold above coin value, $157.10 a share today, or preferred sold to buy coin. Preferred-funded coin raises the company's measure at once, but it adds no net coin per share when it is issued, because the new claim offsets the new coin. It adds net coin only if bitcoin then rises faster than the coupon, and that is a forecast of bitcoin. The company's objective of doubling BPS, about 10.4% a year, rests on the second route. Phong Le described the plan on the call: digital credit is the primary way to acquire bitcoin, and the idea is to issue common stock above 1x mNAV to pay the dividends and interest on that credit. At 1.14× on our basis (1.16× on the company's) that common would be sold above NAV but below coin value. On its own, the dividend leg is the "new common" path above: bitcoin per share falls 2.5% a year at today's prices. The credit leg's coin raises bitcoin per share when it is bought, and raises net coin per share only if that coin outruns its coupon. Since June the company has not issued any preferred.

The stack

The stack: a smaller fixed charge, pre-funded, at lower strike prices

The buybacks changed the cost of the stack as well as its size. Strategy has bought back 20.98 million STRC shares this year at an average $97.39, retiring $2.10 billion of notional at about a 2.6% discount. At a 12% coupon on $100, each buyback retires a claim yielding 12.3% at the purchase price, while the quarter's interest income earned about 4.5% annualized on the company's cash and short-term investments. Those buybacks removed $252 million a year of dividends. At the preferred's notional amounts on 25 October ($13.35 billion, the company's figures by series) and its stated rates, the preferred stack costs $1,007 million a year for STRC and $468 million for the other four series. With $35 million of convertible interest, the total fixed charge is $1.51 billion a year. That is $3.57 a share, against the $1.76 billion management gave in July and the $1.55 billion the June note used. In bitcoin it is 19,300 coins a year, 2.3% of the stack at today's price, from about 3.3% in July.

Two features of the stack changed without changing its cost. From 1 November STRC has a dividend record date on every calendar day, and STRF, STRK and STRD follow on 1 January 2027. The 29 October 8-K states that the change "does not increase or decrease the total amount of regular dividends payable". It changes when the reserve pays, not how much. The return-of-capital guidance rests on the company not expecting positive earnings and profits, a regular-tax measure, for ten years or more. Separately, the Q2 10-Q says that under Treasury's interim guidance of September 2025 the company plans to exclude unrealized bitcoin gains when computing income for the corporate alternative minimum tax, and does not expect to become subject to that tax because of them. Neither change enters the NAV.

Whether STRC stays at par at 12% is the question of The Last Moat Is the Coupon (17 August), and its tripwire is scored in §5. One point bears on this note's valuation. Par has been held this autumn by buybacks paid from USD Cash, as three of the four October 8-Ks state and the fourth's balances show. On the quarter's sources and uses, our inference is that the cash came from the common sold in the third quarter. No new STRC was sold. A preferred franchise has value to the common only when new preferred can be sold at a cost below what the bitcoin it buys earns. So far the franchise has been supported, not sold.

What changed in the method, and why.

  • The preferred at market value, not liquidation preference (+$2.46). A perpetual preferred never matures. Its value to its holders, and so the claim it takes from the common while the company runs, is the present value of its dividends at the market's required yield, which is its price. Liquidation preference binds only in a liquidation, and the common is worth nothing in one only if the claims exceed the assets. The company itself retires preferred at market prices, as the STRC buybacks at $97.39 show. On the 29 October closes (STRF $102.67, STRC $99.99, STRK $70.22, STRD $70.52) and STRE's indicative €78.88 in the company's presentation, the stack is worth $12.37 billion against $13.40 billion of liquidation preference. Liquidation preference stays where a liquidation is the question (the strike and wipe-out) and in the like-for-like exhibits. The scenarios keep it too, because no preferred prices exist for those bitcoin prices.
  • A 21% tax on the unrealized gain (−$1.16). At spot the bitcoin is worth more than its $64.02 billion cost, and a sale would be taxed at the federal rate. The note deducts $0.49 billion at the anchor, and the same rule at each scenario's price: zero in the bear case, below cost, and $7.9 billion in the bull case. No credit is taken for a loss. This replaces the earlier sensitivity that deducted the 30 September deferred tax liability at the 29 October price. The rule is a liability on a sale; the minimum tax is a separate question (§3). On a going-concern view the tax would be discounted for coins the company does not expect to sell. The house deducts it in full because selling is now policy: the company sold bitcoin in each of the last two quarters (5,552 coins in the third), and its board has standing authority to sell bitcoin to fund dividends, buybacks and the reserve. Without the tax, holding the same three converted series, the bull case is $247.52.
  • Employee awards (−$0.90). Options in the money at $141.62, by the treasury-stock method, and the stock units outstanding at 30 June add 3.04 million shares. The third-quarter 10-Q will refresh the count.
  • The convertibles at market value (−$0.57). With the preferred at market, leaving the convertibles at principal would value the two claims on different bases. The company's presentation prices the six series at 23 October (from $90.88 to $125.16 per $100), which gives $6.96 billion against $6.71 billion of principal: four series trade above par on the value of their conversion option, two below. A model of each series, a bond floor of $6.01 billion (2-year Treasury 4.67% plus an assumed 3-point credit spread, to each put date) plus options worth $0.93 billion (Black-Scholes at the stock's one-year realized volatility, 81%), gives $6.95 billion, a close cross-check. The prices are from a day the stock closed $4.39 higher, which overstates the option value slightly.

One adjustment was checked and not made. The 30 September dividends payable ($136 million) were paid from the USD Reserve in October (8-Ks of 5 and 19 October), so the 25 October USD assets are already net of them. Dividends accrued since then sit in the preferred prices, which trade with the accrual.

Scenarios. The June note's three pairs of bitcoin price and premium are kept, applied to today's balances and share count, as brackets rather than forecasts. $32,000 is 74% below bitcoin's closing high of $124,824 (6 October 2025), a shallower fall than the 77% and 84% falls from the 2021 and 2017 highs on the same daily closes. $120,000 is 4% below that high. The bull is not set the same way. On the same closes each cycle's high has exceeded the one before, the last by 1.85× (and the one before by 3.4×), so $120,000 is a bracket near the last high, not a cycle-high case. Above the tax cost each further dollar of bitcoin adds 79 cents of NAV. 0.85× and 1.20× bracket the premium's readings since July: 1.00× at the Q2 note, 1.13× at 30 September on the like-for-like basis and 1.14× at the 29 October close. The tax is deducted at each scenario's bitcoin price, and the outer scenarios carry the preferred at liquidation preference and the convertibles at principal, because no prices exist for those bitcoin prices. In the bull case the three convertible series in the money at that value are treated as converted, which takes it from $226.35 to $225.87. Exhibit 5 — Return on invested capital against its cost, twelve quarters

How to read it. Trailing-twelve-month return on invested capital, percent, December 2023 to September 2026. Invested capital is equity plus debt plus the perpetual preferred, less cash. The dashed line is a 16.9% weighted cost of capital (equity at 20.0%: the ten-year yield plus the stock's own 60-month beta of 3.31 times a 4.5% equity risk premium; preferred at its 11.0% coupon; convertibles at theirs), held flat. Before 2025 bitcoin was carried at impaired cost, so the line shows only losses; from 2025 it is at fair value. Compare the line with the dashed hurdle. The one thing it shows: the return swings with the bitcoin mark. At September it is −22.5%, because the late-2025 and first-half 2026 losses are still in the trailing window.

Return on capital: a measure of bitcoin, not of the business. By the house's mechanical rule the regime is weakness: −22.5% against 16.9%: the spread was +4.6 points a year ago and is −39.4 now, so the return has gone from above its cost to well below it. Strategy's operating income is almost entirely the change in the value of its bitcoin. The quarter's $20.91 billion gain sat beside $89.6 million of software gross profit. So the trailing return is bitcoin's twelve-month move run through the balance sheet, not a spread the business earns over its cost of capital. The label repeats what the NAV already shows at spot. It is recorded because the house requires it for every valuation, and it adds nothing beyond the bitcoin price.

Why this hurdle. The cost of equity uses the stock's own measured risk: Strategy's equity is a geared claim on its own bitcoin, and 60 months of its own returns give the stock's sensitivity to the market (beta 3.31, standard error 0.70). The listed alternatives each import something else. Blending toward Coinbase, MARA and Riot (20.9%) mixes in an exchange's and two miners' operating businesses (trading volumes; power and hardware costs), though the miners also hold bitcoin, far less than Strategy's 848,517 coins. The usual adjustment toward a market-average beta (16.5%) assumes a drift that nothing in the balance sheet implies. A hurdle built from bitcoin itself would mean stating a required return on bitcoin, which the house does not do. The regime label is the same at every one of these rates: September's −22.5% is far below any of them.

What $141.62 requires. At today's balances, NAV equals the price when bitcoin is at $88,986, +13.8% above the close; above the tax cost each dollar of bitcoin gain adds 79 cents of NAV. At today's bitcoin price, the price pays 1.14× NAV: $7.2 billion, or $17.01 a share, above the coin, dollars and software net of every claim.

  3. Preferred issuance resumes. What would change our view

The house would move toward the price, by giving the credit franchise a value above zero, on any two of these. Tripwire 2 met: $1 billion or more of common sold in a quarter above NAV per share, with the proceeds buying bitcoin. Tripwire 3 met: $500 million or more of preferred sold in a quarter at or above its stated amount, with the proceeds buying bitcoin, at a coupon of 12% or less. A quarter in which net coin per share rises at a constant bitcoin price, the measure Exhibit 2 holds at one price, as well as the company's BTC Yield.

The house would move toward the bear case's inputs, by making the base case's premium less than 1.00×, on any two of the mirror conditions. mNAV below 1.00× for ten consecutive sessions (tripwire 5). USD coverage below 2.0 years, refilled by bitcoin sales (tripwire 4). A STRC rate increase above 12%, or STRC back below $97 with the buyback authorization spent. Or another quarter in which more than 80% of the common sold goes to something other than bitcoin while the stock is below coin value, with no preferred sold into bitcoin that quarter. In that quarter bitcoin per share falls again without the claims falling as far. MSCI's deletion decision on 11 November is on neither list. It changes who buys the common, not what the common owns, and it is scored on its own trigger.
