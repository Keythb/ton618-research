Circle (CRCL) — The Token Sale Made the Quarter. The Distribution Bill Sets the Value.

Circle is worth $55.95 a share on our model, against a $75.22 close. The third quarter's revenue growth was a one-time token sale: without it, revenue fell 7.7% and operating income was $26.5 million. The value turns on how reserve income is split with the partners that distribute USDC. Binance's fee moves that split against Circle from the fourth quarter; a higher Fed path and the GENIUS rules work the other way. The price needs USDC to grow about 35% a year from 2028.

TON618 Capital Research · As of 3 November 2026 · Download PDF · Download model

In July we valued Circle at $50 and called retention the decisive variable: the share of reserve income Circle keeps after paying the platforms that hold USDC for their users. Four months on, the stock is at $75.22, third-quarter revenue rose 17%, and the share paid away fell slightly, to 62.2%. Both readings are incomplete. Most of the growth was a sale of ARC tokens that happens once, and the company's guide says the fourth quarter brings a step down in what Circle keeps, as Binance's new fee runs for a full quarter. Against that, the Fed raised rates in September and projects 4.1% through 2027, which lifts the income on USDC's reserves. This note re-prices Circle on all three.

Terms used throughout, defined once. USDC: Circle's US-dollar stablecoin, a token redeemable one for one in dollars and backed by cash and short-term Treasuries. Reserve income: the interest Circle earns on those reserves. Reserve return rate: reserve income as a yield on average USDC. Distribution ratio: distribution and transaction costs as a share of reserve income, the part paid to Coinbase, Binance and other partners that hold or promote USDC. RLDC: revenue less distribution costs, Circle's measure of what it keeps. ARC: the token of Arc, Circle's own blockchain; the presale sold 807.5 million of 10 billion tokens to institutions at $0.30. Proof of stake: a system in which token holders lock up tokens to run the network and earn rewards. GENIUS Act: the 2025 US law that regulates payment stablecoins; its rules take effect on 18 January 2027. Cost of equity: the annual return a shareholder should require. FCFE: free cash flow to equity.

The case for the price

The case for the price, in the company's terms

The strongest reading of this quarter comes from Circle's own filings and call. It goes like this.

  1. USDC is winning the use that matters. USDC carried 79% of all stablecoin onchain transaction volume in the third quarter, from 66% a year earlier, on about 27% of supply. Onchain volume rose 49% to $13.9 trillion, a record $208 billion was minted and redeemed, and meaningful wallets rose 16%. On management's argument, balances follow usage, so circulation lags but follows.
  2. The rules arrive in January, and they came out friendlier. The GENIUS rules take effect on 18 January 2027. The OCC's final rule of 21 October dropped the proposed presumption that partner reward arrangements pay prohibited yield. Management says large companies and banks have been waiting for exactly this before building on digital dollars.
  3. Distribution is being bought on purpose, at the best venues. Binance (more than 300 million registered users) signed for five years and invested $100 million in Circle stock. Under the Hyperliquid arrangement, that venue's USDC balances sit on Coinbase's platform. Circle calls these investments designed to grow circulation and RLDC dollars over time, and paid no upfront cost for Binance.
  4. The rest of the platform is scaling fast. Circle Payments Network (CPN) reached $41.3 billion of annualized payment volume at quarter-end and more than $58 billion by 30 October, from $23 billion at 31 July. Tazapay adds $25 billion of volume and payout rails in more than 100 markets. Management expects material CPN revenue in 2027.
  5. Arc is live, and Circle owns a quarter of it. Mainnet launched on 16 September with 11 founding validators and more than 100 institutions and applications; about $1.5 billion of USDC, cirBTC and EURC sat on Arc by late October. The presale valued the network at $3 billion. Circle's 25% allocation, 2.5 billion tokens, is carried at nil under GAAP and would be worth $750 million at the presale price.
  6. Rates went up, not down. The Fed raised its target in September, and its projections put the policy rate at 4.1% through 2027. On Circle's filed sensitivity, each 100 basis points on the reserve yield is worth about $368 million a year to Circle after distribution.

What this note tests, and what it cannot. It tests claims 2, 3, 5 and 6 directly: the rule text, the fourth-quarter guide's arithmetic on distribution, the ARC terms in the 10-Q, and the rate path against Circle's own sensitivity. It tests claim 1 against the float, which is what earns reserve income, and claim 4 against the revenue line it feeds. It cannot test whether usage turns into balances on any timetable: no filed data link the two, and the 40% multi-year growth framework the company keeps is not a forecast with a date. The bull case carries that claim at close to the company's pace.

The print

What the quarter earned

Total revenue and reserve income was $864.9 million, up 17% on the year. Of that, $181.8 million was ARC presale revenue: about 75% of the $242.2 million presale, recognized as the tokens were minted and delivered and the public mainnet launched. Excluding it, revenue was $683.1 million, down 7.7%. Reserve income fell 8% to $657.6 million: average USDC rose 8% to $73.3 billion, but the reserve return rate fell 61 basis points to 3.5%. Other revenue excluding ARC was $25.5 million, down 11%, after $41.6 million in the first quarter and $33.6 million in the second; the company cited fewer blockchain integrations and lower subscription and services revenue.

The margin expansion is the presale. RLDC was $453.3 million, a 52.4% margin, which the release shows as 1,291 basis points up on the year. Excluding the presale, which carries no distribution cost, RLDC was $271.5 million, a 39.7% margin, against 39.5% a year earlier. Operating expenses rose 16% to $244.9 million, so operating income of $208.3 million becomes $26.5 million without the presale, against $81.0 million a year earlier, and that is before any cost the presale itself carried.

Net income was taxed at about 1%. Income tax was $2.2 million on $228.2 million of pre-tax income: federal and state loss carryforwards and stock-compensation deductions shelter US income, and Circle keeps a valuation allowance on some US tax assets. Net income was $226.0 million, $0.84 a diluted share. Other income fell to $19.9 million from $72.1 million, which a year ago included a $56.2 million fair-value gain. We value every year at a 25% tax rate. The federal loss carryforward ($478.8 million at the end of 2025) would add at most $0.36 a share at the 21% federal rate, less whatever 2026's income has used, so we leave it out.

Adjusted EBITDA has two definitions, and the presale dominates both. Adjusted EBITDA was $316.7 million, up 85%. Since the first quarter it also excludes employer payroll taxes on stock compensation, which adds $2.7 million this quarter ($313.9 million on the prior definition) and $5.0 million a year ago. That change is small; the presale is not: excluding it, adjusted EBITDA was $134.9 million, down 21% on the year.

The split

The split, the float and the rate

The fourth-quarter guide says the split moves against Circle. The company kept its full-year guide: other revenue $310–330 million and an RLDC margin of 41.7–43.7%, both including the presale. The CFO added the figures that matter. Excluding ARC, the full-year margin should land "near the midpoint of our original guidance range" of 38–40%, and Q4 is the first full quarter of both the Hyperliquid arrangement and the renewed Binance partnership. He pointed analysts to "the step down that we anticipate in Q4". Nine-month RLDC margin excluding ARC was 40.8%. With fourth-quarter reserve income of about $680 million (average USDC of $72.5 billion at a 3.75% reserve rate) and other revenue at the midpoint of the $130–150 million full-year range excluding ARC, a 39% full year needs a fourth-quarter margin of about 34%. That is a distribution ratio of about 69.6%, against 62.2% in the third quarter; at 40% it is 65.5%, at 38% 73.7%. Put another way: holding the third quarter's terms and allowing for the higher rate, Circle would keep about $233 million a year more than the guide implies, about 32 basis points on the float. The guide was restated five weeks into the quarter, with the cause named.

Coinbase renewed on the same terms; the cost comes through other partners. Payments to Coinbase were $331.3 million, 50.4% of reserve income (50.8% a year earlier), and distribution costs fell 8.5% on the year on lower reserve income. USDC on Circle's own platform rose 36% to $13.9 billion, 18.1% of circulation on a daily average (+462 basis points), and Circle keeps all the reserve income on those balances. That mix did not lower the ratio by much in the third quarter, and the guide adds a new payer in the fourth. July's bear case put the ratio at 68% partly on a worse Coinbase renewal. Coinbase renewed on the same terms (its own third-quarter 10-Q says so; under Circle's agreement a renewal runs three years, which would run to August 2029), yet the fourth-quarter ratio our arithmetic on the guide implies is above July's bear.

What we use. The base case holds the ratio at 69.6% at a 3.75% reserve rate: the fourth-quarter ratio our arithmetic on the guide implies, held flat, because Binance's fee is in the run-rate from that quarter and no filed figure or guide puts 2027 below it. Circle argues that the new agreements grow circulation and RLDC dollars, and its own-platform share rose 462 basis points in a year. For a year, though, new incentive arrangements have offset that mix: the ratio moved only from 62.9% to 62.2%, and the 10-Q says Circle expects distribution expense to increase as it adds distributors. So the base stays flat through and after the 2029 renewal, and the bull case carries the improvement: it starts at 65.5% (the 40% end of the guide) and reaches 62% by 2031 as balances grow outside incentive programs. The bear uses 72% (near the 38% end) and adds two points at the 2029 renewal.

What Circle keeps when rates move. Circle's filed sensitivity says 100 basis points on the reserve yield changes reserve income by $739 million and distribution costs by $371 million, so Circle keeps about half of any rate change, more than the 37.8% of reserve income it kept on average in the third quarter. The model applies that split year by year. It is measured on today's contracts, and the Coinbase and Binance terms renew in 2029 and 2031: if Circle keeps only 37.8% of a rate change from 2030, fair value is $58.70; at 61.8%, $53.35. The effect is small, and it runs against intuition because the long-run reserve rate of about 3.07% (the 3.2% policy rate less the 13bp gap) sits below the 3.75% reference: a smaller share of a rate change is also a smaller share of the fall.

The rate path is higher than July assumed. July's base case used a 3.0% reserve rate for 2027 on expected Fed cuts. Instead, the Fed raised its target to 3.75–4.00% in September and held it in October, with two members voting for another rise; the median projection is 4.1% at the end of 2026 and 2027, 3.9% in 2028, 3.6% in 2029 and 3.2% in the long run. Circle's reserve rate has run about 13 basis points below effective fed funds (3.54% against 3.67% in the third quarter), so the base case earns 3.97% in 2027 and 3.07% from 2030. The bear uses the low end of the Fed's projections and the bull the top of its central tendency.

The float is flat, and October gave back the summer. USDC ended September at $73.9 billion, flat on the year, and its share of US-dollar stablecoins fell 183 basis points to 27%. It rose in August and September, then fell to $71.9 billion at the end of October and $71.3bn on 3 November, below June. All US-dollar stablecoins on DeFiLlama's count are about $295 billion, down 3% in twelve months; Circle's 27% share uses a narrower CoinMarketCap set. Open USD, the consortium coin that Coinbase backs, had about $0.9 billion outstanding in early November, 0.3% of the market; Circle's 10-Q does not mention it. The company's 40% multi-year growth framework is unchanged, and the CFO said growth "will not follow a straight line".

The ARC position is its own asset. A token sale is revenue when recognized, and it does not recur: the rest of the presale ($60.4 million) sits in deferred revenue against an obligation the 10-Q ties to Arc's move to proof of stake, and further sales depend on Circle choosing to sell. So we take ARC out of every earnings run-rate and value the position once. Circle's allocation is 25% of the 10 billion tokens; the whitepaper puts 60% in an ecosystem pool that covers token sales, grants and growth programs, so we treat the presale as coming from that pool. The base case values Circle's 2.5 billion tokens plus 0.5 billion of future ecosystem sales at the $0.30 presale price, discounted three years at the cost of equity (proof of stake in 2027, then at least a year of lock-up by analogy with the presale buyers' terms) and taxed at 25% on a nil basis, less a 10% chance that buyers use their right to be repaid if Arc has not moved to proof of stake by 8 May 2028: $444 million. The mark is not independent: an affiliate of IDG Capital, a holder of more than 5% of Circle, bought $25 million of the presale, and the token does not trade. The bear values the tokens at zero and assumes half the presale is repaid (−$121 million); the bull values 4 billion tokens at $1.00 ($2,079 million). Staking rewards, which the CFO named as Circle's main expected recurring revenue from Arc, are paid in ARC, so they sit in the token's value, not a separate line.

The value

What Circle is worth after the print, and why the number moved

How we value it. Circle earns a spread on money that is not its own: the $73.6 billion of reserve cash and the $73.5 billion owed to USDC holders match each other and sit outside capital, and most of its $3.86 billion of equity is corporate liquidity of $2.97 billion. So we value free cash flow to equity driver by driver from 2027 to 2040, with growth landing on 4.0% (3.0% in the bear case), the Fed's long-run projections of 2.0% real growth plus 2.0% inflation, and count the liquidity once, at face. Operating capital is what remains, about $0.9 billion, including $0.7 billion of goodwill and intangibles from acquisitions, which count because they were bought.

The bear case is close to the liquidity floor of $10.75 a share: it loses money in 2027, and we assume management then cuts costs to stay near break-even, as it did with a 5% workforce reduction in the third quarter. The bull case caps the operating margin at 60% of RLDC, between Mastercard's 57.6% and Visa's 61.2% operating margins in their latest fiscal years; it binds from 2031. At 67% fair value is $59.84; with no cap, $65.75. Terminal value is 60% of the base case and 55% of the bull's operating value.

The cost of equity. The rate is a judgment about Circle's risk now. Sixteen monthly returns since the June 2025 listing give an own beta of 1.07 with a standard error of 2.07. Taken at face value that gives about 9.8%, close to the rate the price implies, but a standard error that wide cannot tell a beta of 1 from a beta of 3, and the decline after the IPO dominates it. So the peers set the read, and choosing them is the judgment. Reserve income behaves like a rate-sensitive float, which points to payments and broker reads (Visa 0.79, Interactive Brokers 1.33, PayPal 1.38); balances that move with the crypto cycle and high fixed costs point to Coinbase (3.17), Robinhood (2.37) and Block (2.56). The six-name median of 1.87 gives 13.4% on a 4.95% ten-year Treasury yield and a 4.5% equity risk premium. We use a beta of 1.8, slightly under it, because income tied to short-term rates argues against the top of the crypto-side reads: 13.0%. The terminal rate is 10.4%, a beta of 1.2, at the top of the stable range, because a mature USDC still carries rate and crypto-cycle exposure. July stated no rate; its multiple implied about 15.9%, a beta near 2.4, between Robinhood's 2.37 and Block's 2.56. The peer median gives $54.88; one point either way on the explicit rate gives $58.79 and $53.32.

The weights. We keep 25 / 50 / 25, as a decision on its own condition. The evidence since July pulls both ways. The float was flat and the split moved against Circle, which is the bear case's direction; the rate path rose and the GENIUS rules arrive in January with a friendlier final text, which is the bull's. Nothing observable today favors one tail over the other, so equal tails stand. A tilt of five points of weight toward either tail gives $48.81 or $63.10; the tripwires below say what would move them.

From July's $50. July's engine, re-run unchanged, gives $49.76 (published as $50): 2027 net income at a 25% tax rate times 35 (base) or 38 (bull), plus corporate liquidity, with the bear at 2.5 times gross profit, weighted 25 / 50 / 25 on 285 million shares. Exhibit 3 moves one input at a time to today's value on that engine. Liquidity at 30 September, including the presale cash and Binance's $100 million, adds +$1.67. A smaller float takes away $26.04 (2027 average USDC of $65 / $77 / $92 billion, against July's $60 / $95 / $150 billion), and the Fed's higher path adds +$20.82. The distribution ratio with Binance and Hyperliquid in the run-rate takes away $6.00. Other revenue excluding ARC takes away $11.14: July's base carried $320 million for 2027 against an annualized $102 million today. Costs add +$2.50, and the ARC position adds +$2.50. On July's method, the quarter's data give $34.06.

Then the method. July used 285 million shares to allow for dilution while also counting stock pay as a cost, which charges it twice; today's diluted count plus the Tazapay stock, 276.2 million, with stock pay as a cost, adds +$1.08. On our engine July's whole method equals a flat cost of equity of 15.9%: a multiple of 35 on earnings this close to a trough is the same as requiring 15.9% a year from the stage model's cash flows. At that rate the switch of engine is neutral by construction, so the change from July is the rate. Moving it to the argued 13.0% in every year adds +$10.45, and letting it converge to 10.4% from 2031 to 2038 as USDC matures adds +$10.36. The result is $55.95.

What the price requires. At $75.22, the weighted value equals the price only at a flat cost of equity of 9.4%, about what an average stock with a beta of 1 requires. Holding our rates, the base case needs USDC to grow about 35% a year from 2028 to 2031, to about $256 billion, against 18% in our base case and about 10% a year from peak to peak. Or it needs the base case's distribution ratio at about 45%, a smaller share for partners than in any year Circle has reported (50.3% in 2023). Or the bull case needs about 41% of the weight, with the bear kept at 25%. Two routes come closer: if every case's adoption stage lasts two years longer, to 2033, the weighted value is $78.41, above the price; if the policy rate holds at 4.1% for good instead of falling to 3.2%, it is $71.84. In July's own frame, a 35-times multiple on 2027 earnings, the price needs about $122 billion of average USDC in 2027, 71% more than today; July's figure was $124 billion, and the float has not moved toward it.

Circle's return on capital is falling toward its cost. It was 25.5% in September 2025, 30.0% in December and 14.4% in September 2026, against a WACC of 13.0%: a fading regime. The cause is operating income, not capital: capital stayed near $0.85 billion while quarterly operating income excluding ARC fell from $81 million to $27 million, as rates fell faster than the float grew and costs rose 16%. Including the presale, the September figure is 31%. If the fourth quarter lands on the guide's midpoint, the trailing return falls to about 8%, below the WACC. Five quarters are all the filings support, because equity less liquidity was negative before the June 2025 IPO. The WACC is the cost of equity to within a basis point, because Circle has no borrowing and its only debt-like claim is $17 million of leases. The terminal value assumes 25% on new capital, below the 2025–26 returns and well above the cost, because growing the float takes little capital but competition limits the spread.

What would change our view

The view moves toward the price if the float and the split improve together: average USDC at or above $92 billion in any 2027 quarter, the bull case's own 2027 average, with the distribution ratio at or below 66%. That is the bull case's path arriving on schedule, and half the weight would move to it, giving $91.68. The view moves away if both worsen together: average USDC at or below $65 billion, the bear case's own 2027 average, in any 2027 quarter with the ratio at or above 72%, which would put half the weight on the bear case, giving $20.23. Short of either, the single readings in the tripwires apply: the fourth-quarter ratio (settled at the February print), the 2027 other-revenue guide (same call), average USDC in the second quarter of 2027, and whether Arc reaches proof of stake by 8 May 2028.

The call

The call

We value Circle at $55.95 a share, 26% below the $75.22 close, with a range of $9.71 to $152.60. The quarter's growth was a token sale, and the guide says the split of reserve income moves against Circle as Binance's fee runs in full. Against that, rates are higher than July assumed, the GENIUS rules arrive in January, Arc is live, and Circle holds a token position July could not value. Measured on July's method, the rise from $50 comes from the required return, not from the quarter: July's multiple implied about 15.9% a year, and we argue 13.0% falling to 10.4%. The price asks for USDC growth of about 35% a year for four years, roughly double our base case. The next decisive readings both come at the February print: the fourth-quarter distribution ratio and the 2027 guide.
