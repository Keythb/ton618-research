Goldman Sachs (GS) — Built for Bull Markets, Priced for a Permanent One

Goldman Sachs earned 22.4% on its tangible equity in the first nine months of 2026, a return beaten only by 2021 in the fifteen years of the series, and it is built for markets like this one. Among the five big US trading banks it has had the largest share of advisory fees every year since 2015 and has won share in equities since 2017, including in two of the three down years since 2018; its trading and investment-banking revenue moves about 1.2 times as much as theirs, and this year it kept 53 cents of pre-tax profit from each extra dollar of revenue. Keep that franchise, return the market to its trend and add the 2028 capital step, and Goldman earns about 16%, five points above its cost of equity; most of the rest of this year's return is a market running a quarter above trend. At $892, 2.6 times tangible book on the 13 October close, the shares need more than this year's market, forever, at an 11% cost of equity. Fair value $602 a share, 32.5% below the last trade; the proponent's own best case $796.

On Tuesday 13 October Goldman Sachs reported net revenues of $18.7 billion, its second-highest quarter, earnings of $17.01 a share against a consensus of $13.05, and an annualized return on tangible common equity of 20.5%. Advisory fees, equities financing and asset-management fees all set records, and the firm raised its full-year alternatives fundraising expectation to about $150 billion from a plan of $75–100 billion. The shares opened 3% higher and closed slightly down, at $891.91. Asked whether the returns were cyclical or structural, the chief executive said both: "We're in a top quartile environment," which, he said, flatters the average return through the cycle, but "structurally" the firm's base returns "have been meaningfully uplifted over the last five years." This note is about how much of each.

Terms used throughout, defined once. ROTCE / ROTE: return on tangible common equity, net income to common shareholders over equity less preferred stock, goodwill and intangibles; the return the firm earns on the capital it holds. Goldman reports it on monthly-average equity ("ROTE"); the fifteen-year history here uses year-end averages. TBV: tangible book value, that same capital; $341.43 a share at 30 September. P/TBV: price over tangible book. Cost of equity (CoE): the return shareholders require; built here as the ten-year Treasury plus the stock's beta times an equity risk premium. Excess return: ROTCE minus the cost of equity, earned on tangible book; a firm earning exactly its cost of equity is worth book. Terminal: the level a quantity settles at after the explicit forecast; the fade or glide is its path there. FICC: Fixed Income, Currency and Commodities — the trading business in interest-rate products, credit, mortgages, currencies and commodities; with Equities, it makes up the markets business inside Global Banking & Markets. Intermediation: market-making in FICC and equities — buying and selling for clients, earning spreads and commissions and managing the risk held in between. Financing: lending to markets clients against collateral — repo, structured and mortgage lending in FICC; prime brokerage and portfolio financing in Equities. Durable revenues: Goldman's own term for its financing and fee lines (§4). GBM / AWM / PS: the three segments — Global Banking & Markets, Asset & Wealth Management, Platform Solutions (the Apple Card, being transferred to another issuer). CET1: common equity tier 1 ratio, core capital over risk-weighted assets; Goldman's requirement is 11.4% and management runs 50–100 basis points above it. SLR: supplementary leverage ratio, capital over total exposure without risk weights. G-SIB surcharge: the extra capital a global systemically important bank must hold, 3.5% for Goldman now. The five-bank pool: the combined trading and investment-banking fee revenue of Goldman, JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley, Bank of America and Citigroup (§4). Advisory / underwriting: the two halves of investment-banking fees — fees for advice on mergers and other transactions, and fees for selling clients' new shares and bonds.

The other side, first

The case for the price

The strongest case that $892 is right, in the terms its proponents would use.

  1. The uplift is structural, and the record proves it. Since the 2020 Investor Day revenue is up roughly 60% (the shareholder letter's figure, about 8% a year; 11% a year since 2022 on the chief executive's count), durable revenues have doubled, historical principal investments have been cut from $64 billion to $6 billion, and the stress capital buffer has fallen 320 basis points. The new regime's trough year, 2023, with about $3 billion of consumer and principal-investment losses inside it, still earned 8%, a level the old regime fell below in three of its ten years. A model that fades the return toward a fifteen-year mean that includes 2011, 2015 and 2017 is averaging two different firms.
  2. Financing is a growth business with a cycle, not a cyclical business. FICC and Equities financing have grown from $2.5 billion a quarter to $4.6 billion in two years and are now more than 40% of markets revenue. The growth in tradable assets, prime balances and Asia, where demand "continues to exceed available industry capacity", is secular. Spreading the firm's average costs over these lines, as this note does, understates their margin: they use balance sheet and risk appetite, not headcount.
  3. Asset & Wealth Management is an asset manager inside a bank multiple. Record management fees of $3.6 billion a quarter on $4.1 trillion of supervised assets, 35 straight quarters of long-term inflows, wealth inflows running at 12% a year against a 5% target, $150 billion of alternatives fundraising against a $75–100 billion plan, targets of a 17–19% return and a 30% pre-tax margin — and a return on its own attributed equity of 14.4% in the nine months, above the cost of equity. BlackRock trades at about 25 times trailing GAAP earnings. Goldman's fee earnings deserve that multiple, and a bank-style excess-return model cannot express it.
  4. The compensation ratio has reset. 29.6% of revenue this year (30% on the company's net-of-provisions basis), against 31–34% in 2022–25 (30% in the 2020–21 boom) and 37% a decade ago, because the growth is in balance-sheet and platform businesses that do not scale headcount. Operating leverage is the structural reason returns stay above the old band.
  5. Nearly all earnings are being returned, and the buyback shrinks the count. The firm paid out 96% of nine-month earnings to common shareholders, and basic shares fell from 307 million to 296 million in nine months, so earnings per share compound faster than earnings.
  6. The cost of equity is lower than 11.0%. The Blume-adjusted beta gives 10.7%; the JPMorgan valuation uses 10.5%; a 4% equity risk premium rather than 4.8% gives 10.1%. At the lowest of these the return the price requires falls toward 21%, and the proponent's case comes within a few percent of the price.
  7. Goldman is taking share, and share compounds. Its part of the five big US trading banks' trading and investment-banking revenue has risen from 18.7% in 2017 to 24.8%, the highest in the series. In equities it has led the five since 2021 and gained share in two of the three years that market shrank since 2018. A firm that keeps winning share does not revert to its old mean; it moves the mean.

Of these, this note tests (1) in §3 and §4, where the fifteen-year, post-2020 and best-year returns are laid against the target and the requirement, and the base case rests at the band's midpoint, set by the peer test in §4; (2) in §4, where the durable lines' stability and contribution are computed from the segment tables, with the allocation run both ways; (3) in §6, where AWM is priced at 13–19 times fee earnings and each multiple's implied perpetual growth is printed; (4) in §2 and §5, where the compensation ratio is reported and sensitized; (5) in §7, where the payout the model needs is reconciled to the buyback and the capital requirement's path; and (6) in §3 and §5, where the cost of equity is built and every result is shown on a grid, with the proponent's case at the Blume beta; and (7) in §4, where the share gain is measured against the five-bank pool, split into what the cycle explains and what it does not, and the base case holds the 2026 share rather than reverting it. It cannot test whether 2026's top-quartile environment is the new normal, which only the next several years can settle; whether the share gains continue; or the durable lines' true margin, which the firm does not publish.

How it got here

How Goldman got here, and where it says it is going

23consecutive years as the #1 M&A adviser, by the firm's own count; $1.6tn advised in 2025, more than $250bn ahead of the closest peer

From partnership to bank. Founded in 1869 and a partnership until its 1999 listing, Goldman became a bank holding company on Sunday 21 September 2008, approved together with Morgan Stanley so that both could reach the Federal Reserve's window. It has spent the years since deciding what kind of bank to be. It built a consumer deposit base under the Marcus name, then retreated from consumer lending: the GreenSky and Marcus loan books were sold from 2023, the General Motors card in 2025, and in December 2025 the Apple Card was agreed for transfer to another issuer, a two-year transition the firm says "will substantially complete the narrowing" of that focus. Assets went from $1.1 trillion at the end of 2008, as later recast, to $1.8 trillion at the end of 2025 and $2.2 trillion now. The 1MDB settlements of 2020 left an asset-recovery guarantee to Malaysia that is still in arbitration.

The scorecard. The 2020 Investor Day set the targets the firm measures itself against, and the 2025 shareholder letter keeps the score: earnings per share from $21.03 in 2019 to $51.32 in 2025, book value per share from $218.52 to $357.60, return on equity from 10.0% to 15.0%, total shareholder return of 341% since 2019. The letter restates the through-the-cycle targets and the intention "in the near term, [to] exceed them — though I know our journey will not be a straight line."

The landscape, as five forces.

  • Rivalry. Advice is the one place where share behaves like a moat: #1 in M&A for 23 consecutive years on the firm's count, $1.6 trillion advised in 2025, more than $250 billion ahead of the next adviser, and, by its own account, $5 billion more advisory revenue than the #2 since 2020. On the five big US trading banks' own filings it has had the largest share of their advisory fees every year since 2015 (§4). In markets the rivals are JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley, Citigroup and Bank of America; on June tangible book, Goldman trades at 2.6 times and Morgan Stanley at 3.5. In wealth, Morgan Stanley is the mirror image, with wealth revenues of $31.8 billion at a 29% margin in 2025 on $10 trillion of client assets, against Goldman's $2.1 trillion in an ultra-high-net-worth market it calls "very, very fragmented" with "no big leader". The listed alternative managers — Blackstone, KKR, Apollo, BlackRock — compete with it in private credit and are its clients in prime financing.
  • Buyers. Hedge funds and sponsors on one side, corporates on the other. Equities financing demand in Asia "continues to exceed available industry capacity", in a market the chief financial officer calls "supply-of-financing constrained", so pricing power sits with the firm for now; sponsors, "slow to monetize" their 2020–21 vintages, hold the option to wait.
  • Suppliers. Talent, at a 30% compensation ratio and "a very, very strong bid for the very, very best people"; and capital, priced by the Federal Reserve — a requirement that rises from 11.4% to 11.9% in 2028 and possibly 12.4% from 2029, and a leverage ratio that caps how much financing the balance sheet can carry.
  • Substitutes. Private credit for bank lending, which Goldman answers by raising it itself (about $150 billion of alternatives this year); the private investment-grade market and the hyperscalers' own cash for public debt in AI financing; electronification, which it absorbed in equities.
  • Entrants. The new rails — tokenization, agentic trading, decentralized-finance vaults, prediction markets — which management treats as an expansion of its market rather than a threat (§8).

Where management says it is going. Durable revenues at about 45% of the total as the base. Alternatives fundraising of $75–100 billion a year "on a sustainable basis" (about $150 billion in 2026), $750 billion of alternatives under supervision by 2030 and $300 billion of credit alternatives by the end of 2028. Asset & Wealth Management to a 17–19% return and a 30% pre-tax margin within three to five years of 2025, on fee and private-banking revenue that has compounded 12% a year since 2021, with wealth inflows of 5% a year (12% now). AI-infrastructure financing — "north of $10 trillion of investment through 2032" — as the next cycle, which the firm intends to advise, originate, distribute and fund from public and private pools. Asia, now 20% of revenue from 13% a year ago. One Goldman Sachs 3.0, which the letter calls "our new operating model propelled by AI". Consumer finance out. And the growth it does not promise, as transcribed: "I do not believe we're going to grow our revenues by 11% compounded on the top line for the next five years." The house reads the plan as a franchise built for active markets: its position is durable, and its trading and investment-banking revenue still moves with the market, more than its peers' does. §3 to §6 price both.

The print and the call

What the quarter and the call said

What the quarter said.

  1. Equities financing (a record, +96%) and advisory (a record, +18%) carried a 30% earnings beat. FICC intermediation fell 7% and the backlog ended the quarter lower than it began, though "our backlog remains very robust" and "there's an enormous amount of dialogue that continues".
  2. Sequential financing growth slowed. The finance chief put it at about 3%, which matches the release's equities financing line (FICC financing fell 1%, the two together rose about 2%), and tied the slowdown to deleveraging in the quarter and the market backdrop; year on year, equities financing was up 96%.
  3. The $368 million provision reflected portfolio growth and impairments on "a couple of different names", which the chief financial officer called "highly idiosyncratic", adding that it is not "necessarily the signal of something else to come"; 85% of gross loans are secured.
  4. The compensation ratio is 30% and management intends to hold it — "our best estimate at the moment for what we expect for the year" — with no "diminution in terms of the market for talent. Frankly, quite the opposite."
  5. CET1 fell to 12.4%, the top of the 50–100 basis-point buffer, as risk-weighted assets grew $26 billion; the buyback slowed for a second quarter ($5.0, $4.0, $3.0 billion) while the dividend rose 25%.
  6. Alternatives fundraising guidance rose from $75–100 billion to about $150 billion for the year: "there's nothing opportunistic about it". Incentive fees of $587 million came from clients' "early access to a high-growth investment opportunity", recognized "following the company's IPO", and are expected to "remain elevated in the fourth quarter" — the event-driven component §4 returns to its average.
  7. The environment, in management's words, has gone from "top decile" in May and June to "a top quartile environment" now — "that's still really, really good".

Where management puts the risk, in its own words. The shareholder letter: "we know from history that nothing is linear over time … concerns about private credit, including underwriting quality or exposure to software companies that may be adversely affected by AI, are a reminder that the credit cycle has not been repealed." The call put geopolitics first: the Middle East, energy flows, inflation, bond yields, and a Federal Reserve that hiked for the first time in three years, with markets "pricing in approximately 75 basis points of additional tightening in this cycle across the U.S., U.K., and Europe". On AI financing, the cycle the firm puts first, the chief executive gave the most hedged answer of the morning — "I don't know and nobody knows … There is risk to pressure on … crowding out" — then affirmed the demand: "there's a lot of demand for compute at the moment. The supply is not coming to balance. More is going to get built, more financing is going to be necessary. There will be a point in time when this recalibrates, resets for sure."

What went unraised. Neither management nor the analysts took up the securities book and its marks, deposit betas, lending to nonbank financial institutions (other collateralized loans, $113 billion of the $276 billion of loans; §7), the timing of the Apple Card transfer, or the run-down of the historical principal investments ($6.0 billion at year-end, from $64 billion).

The insiders. Officers and directors sold $149 million of stock in the open market over the last twelve months and bought none, almost all of it between January and May at about $920–980 a share, an average near $950 and roughly 2.8 times today's tangible book; six later sales, about $1 million, were at up to $1,150. It is stated here as a fact rather than a signal.

The price and the record

The price and the record

23.9%the return on tangible equity the price requires in perpetuity, at an 11.0% cost of equity and 3% growth

The identity. The quarter showed what Goldman earns now; the price is a statement about what it earns forever. Price to tangible book is a statement about return: P/TBV = (ROTCE − g) / (CoE − g). At $891.91 and $341.43 of tangible book a share the multiple is 2.61×. With growth at 3% and an 11.0% cost of equity (built below), that is a perpetual return of 23.9%; at 10% it is 21.3%, at 12% 26.5%.

The record. Over fifteen fiscal years (net income to common over average tangible common equity, from the SEC filings; Exhibit 1 shows each) the mean is 11.4% and the median 11.3%, against 22.4% for the first nine months of 2026 on the company's basis; 9.8% in the decade before the Investor Day, 14.6% in the five completed years since (15.9% with the 2026 run-rate). The 10-K target is "through-the-cycle … ROE within a range of 14% to 16% and ROTE within a range of 15% to 17%". The price needs about seven points more than the top of that range.

The cost of equity, in the open. Ten-year Treasury 5.22% (8 October); beta 1.21 on five years of weekly returns against the S&P 500 (262 weeks), 1.14 Blume-adjusted, 1.41 over the last two years; an equity risk premium of 4.8%, the house assumption for financials, shared with the JPMorgan valuation published alongside this one. CoE = 5.22% + 1.21 × 4.8% = 11.0%; 10.7% on the Blume beta, 12.0% on the two-year. Two cross-checks: the line the market draws through twenty-one large banks (§5) values a bank at book when it earns 10.3%, the market's own cost of equity for the group (8.2% on the line through the banks alone); and the FDIC's all-bank return on equity has run 11–13% for five years. JPMorgan's five-year beta on the same method is 1.0, a 10.0% cost of equity, and its valuation uses 10.5%, rounded up from 10.3% on an adjusted two-year beta. Goldman's raw beta runs 0.2–0.3 above JPMorgan's in both windows, worth a point or more; the house takes half of that, because the two-year window that widens the gap most is the one most coloured by 2026. Every result is shown on a 10 / 11 / 12% grid.

The question. At an 11% cost of equity, the price needs more than this year's return, forever. Goldman has not earned that through a cycle — not over fifteen years (11.4%), nor in the five since the Investor Day (14.6%) — and its own through-the-cycle target, 15–17%, does not claim it. What the franchise earns when the market is normal, keeping what it has won, is §4's question, and §5 turns the answer into a value.

Stability, and what the segment tables cannot see. The durable lines rose from $6.1 billion to $9.0 billion over the eight quarters, higher every quarter (quarter-to-quarter standard deviation 4%, peak to trough 1.48); intermediation and investment-banking fees ran from $5.8 billion to $10.9 billion and back to $8.6 billion (20%, 1.90). The durable lines are durable as a trend, not as a level. Spread the firm's nine-month costs evenly across revenue — 60.8% of revenue in costs and provisions, a 20.4% tax rate, $100.5 billion of average tangible equity — and the durable lines contribute 9.9 points of the 22.4% return, intermediation and investment-banking fees 11.2, the rest 1.3. But the even split classes intermediation and advisory as the market because their revenue swings; it cannot say whether Goldman's position in those businesses is itself a franchise. That needs a comparison outside the firm.

Advisory: a lead that holds, not one that widens. Goldman has had the largest advisory share of the five in every one of the eleven years, between 29.1% and 34.1%, against 21–27% for JPMorgan, the runner-up in every year but 2016. The lead has not widened: drift is nil in either window, and Goldman gave share back in four of the five years the advisory pool shrank, three of the four since 2018, 2022 the exception. The position is the franchise; the swings are the market's, amplified. Reputation is one plausible reason the position holds, and the firm's own material describes both sides of it. The chief executive told the July call that the trust behind its advice puts Goldman "in these discussions earlier and alone and without other banks", which he said lets it command more of the wallet; the 2025 shareholder letter counts more than 1.1 million experienced-hire applicants, an internship selection rate below 1% and more than 650 alumni in C-suite roles at companies worth over $1 billion or managers of over $5 billion, and adds that the firms people leave for often become important clients. Equities: share won, including when the market shrank. From 22.3% in 2017 to 28.7% this year; Goldman passed Morgan Stanley in 2021 and has led the five since. Management describes Goldman's role in the AI build-out as advising, structuring, underwriting and distributing, with the private investment-grade funds it manages for clients taking part and certain financing structures kept on its own balance sheet. Deposits: underpaid, and does it matter here? The sector argument is real, and the house makes it in its JPMorgan valuation: $2.08 trillion of interest-bearing deposits paid 2.18% against a 4.23% three-month bill, with $655 billion paying nothing, a gap worth about $70 billion a year to that one bank. It does not transfer to Goldman. Its $568 billion of deposits are consumer savings ($230 billion of Marcus and Apple balances, priced to the market), private-bank balances ($105 billion), transaction-banking operating balances ($93 billion), brokered certificates ($52 billion), sweep balances ($42 billion) and other ($46 billion). Substantially all of it is interest-bearing, and the insured bank's all-in cost of funds was 3.59% at June against a 3.50–3.75% funds target. Goldman's deposit risk is a funding cost that rises with the funds rate, not flight: the money is already paid the market rate. The run question is the other kind — 43% of the bank's deposits are uninsured — and Part 1's run test put Goldman's cover at 1.18 times those deposits from cash, reverse repo and available-for-sale securities, third-highest of eleven. The capital-flight argument belongs to banks that pay 0.07% on checking; it is not a lever in this valuation.

What would change our view

What would change our view. The house would move toward the proponent's case — a resting return of 18% or more, about $670 a share on the base glide — on any of:

  • fiscal 2027 return on tangible equity at or above 20.1% (the bull path's first year) with the five banks' trading and investment-banking revenue at or below its 2026 level, so that the franchise rather than the market is carrying the return — provisionally on the January 2028 print, confirmed if fiscal 2028 repeats it;
  • Goldman's share of the five-bank pool rising in the next year the pool shrinks, the test its share failed in 2017 and 2023 and passed in 2022 — with a sensitivity above one the cycle alone predicts a loss in such a year, so a gain is the franchise beating its own cycle;
  • in the first year after 2026 in which both the five-bank pool and Goldman's net revenues fall, operating expenses falling by at least half the percentage fall in net revenues, so that costs flex with revenue;
  • a disclosed or computable stand-alone return on the durable lines three points or more above the cost of equity;
  • the capital package finalized with Goldman's 2028 G-SIB surcharge held at 3.5%, so that the requirement stays 11.4% and about $4 billion of capital is freed;
  • the ten-year Treasury below 4.2% for two quarters, at which the cost of equity is 10% and the return the price requires falls to 21.3%.

  • fiscal 2027 return on tangible equity at or below 17.7% (the bear path's first year) with the five banks' trading and investment-banking revenue at or above its 2026 level, so that the shortfall is not the cycle — provisionally in January 2028, confirmed if fiscal 2028 repeats it, with a miss in a shrinking market scored base and re-read on fiscal 2028;
  • Goldman's share of the five-bank pool below its 2015–25 average of 21.6% in any fiscal year, the bear case's own anchor;
  • in the first year after 2026 in which both the five-bank pool and Goldman's net revenues fall, operating expenses falling by less than a quarter of the percentage fall in net revenues, or rising; 2022, when they fell 2.4% against a 20.2% fall in revenue, would score here;
  • financing revenue down year on year for two consecutive quarters while equity market capitalization rises;
  • a quarter-end CET1 ratio below 11.9% with repurchases continuing above $2 billion a quarter.

On the parts, the house would raise the Asset & Wealth Management multiple toward 19 if the segment's fiscal 2027 pre-tax margin reaches 27% and its return 15%, and lower it toward 13 if the margin prints at or below 24%.

What the house will score, and when. Nine dated tests, each with a rule for silence; the first, fourth, fifth, sixth, eighth and ninth score conditions in the reversal block above. This is a valuation, not an event pair; Part 2 of the bank thematic scores Part 1's tripwires on this print.

  1. The first year of the fade: fiscal 2027 return on tangible equity, company basis, from the January 2028 release — at or above 20.1% scores the bull path, at or below 17.7% the bear, between them the base, each read with the cycle control above and provisional until fiscal 2028; always printed.
  2. The durable share: fiscal 2026 durable revenues at or above 45% of net revenues and fiscal 2027 durable growth of 8% or more, both together, fire for the franchise; a share below 42% fires for beta; always printed.
  3. The market-beta segment: Global Banking & Markets' fiscal 2027 return on attributed equity at or above 17% (bull) or at or below 13.5% (bear); always printed.
  4. The buffer: a quarter-end standardized CET1 below 11.9% while repurchases exceed $2 billion in the quarter; both print every quarter.
  5. The requirement's path: the G-SIB recalibration finalized by 31 December 2026 with Goldman's 2028 surcharge at or below 3.5% reverses the path; above 3.5% is as modeled; not finalized is scored "slipped" and re-read at the 10-K.
  6. The asset-manager multiple: Asset & Wealth Management's fiscal 2027 pre-tax margin and return (27% and 15% raise the band; 24% or below lowers it); always printed.
  7. The exit: the Apple Card transfer closed by 31 December 2027 with Platform Solutions' attributed equity released below $1 billion; if not, it is scored "slipped" and the segment stays at book with its losses.
  8. The share: Goldman's fiscal 2027 share of the five-bank pool, from the five 10-Ks by 31 March 2028 on this note's definitions — at or above its fiscal 2026 share, computed the same way, with the pool at or below its 2026 level scores the franchise case, because the base expects some give-back as the pool normalizes; below 21.6% scores the bear case in any pool; otherwise the base; always computable.
  9. The cost test: in the first fiscal year after 2026 in which both the pool and Goldman's net revenues fall, operating expenses, which exclude credit provisions, falling by at least half the percentage fall in net revenues scores the franchise case; falling by less than a quarter of it, or rising, scores the bear case, as 2022 (expenses −2.4% against revenue −20.2%) and 2023 (+10.7% against −2.3%, in the year that carried the consumer exit) would have; between, the base. A year in which only the pool falls is not scored, and the test waits for the next; no qualifying year by fiscal 2029 is scored "not yet tested", and headcount against revenue is printed each year instead.

What the note cannot settle. Whether 2026 is the top quartile or the new normal, which only the next several years' returns can show; whether Goldman's share gains continue, and whether the five banks together are gaining on the European banks, which a five-bank pool cannot see; the durable lines' true margin, which the firm does not publish; and whether the market's cost of equity for the group is the 11.0% built here or nearer the 9.9% at which the proponent's case reaches the price.
